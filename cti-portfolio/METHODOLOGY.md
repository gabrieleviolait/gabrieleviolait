# Methodology & Analytic Standards

## Scope

These case studies are designed as professional examples of defensive Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI) work. They combine public-source research with sanitized references to my independent passive monitoring experience.

## Intelligence workflow

1. **Define the Intelligence Requirement** — what decision or knowledge gap the research should address.
2. **Collect** — gather relevant information from authoritative reporting, technical research, actor-controlled sources and historical observations.
3. **Validate** — distinguish independently verifiable facts from adversary claims, reposts, rumours and inference.
4. **Correlate** — connect entities, timestamps, infrastructure, aliases, campaigns, victims, TTPs and external reporting.
5. **Analyse** — identify patterns, alternative explanations and information gaps.
6. **Assess confidence** — state how strongly the available evidence supports the assessment.
7. **Report** — communicate findings in a form useful to technical and non-technical stakeholders.

## Source classes

- **Primary / actor-controlled:** material published by the actor or observed in actor-associated communities and infrastructure.
- **Authoritative:** law-enforcement, CERT/CSIRT, government and official incident-response publications.
- **Technical research:** reputable security-vendor research, telemetry and infrastructure analysis.
- **Secondary reporting:** contextual reporting used only when better primary or technical sources are unavailable.

Actor-controlled material is valuable for intent, messaging and self-reported activity, but a claim is not treated as proof of impact.

## Confidence language

- **High confidence:** supported by multiple independent, reliable sources or strong primary/technical evidence.
- **Moderate confidence:** evidence is credible and consistent but incomplete, indirect or partly dependent on actor-generated material.
- **Low confidence:** limited evidence, unresolved contradictions or significant dependence on unverified claims.

## ATT&CK mapping

MITRE ATT&CK technique IDs are used only when observed or independently documented behaviour supports the mapping. A technique used by one affiliate or campaign is not automatically attributed to every operation associated with a RaaS brand or loose hacktivist ecosystem.

## OPSEC and publication policy

The public portfolio intentionally excludes:

- live onion addresses and access-enabling information;
- credentials or private invitations;
- research identities/personas;
- non-public personal data;
- raw dumps or leaked datasets;
- unnecessary operational details that could facilitate abuse.

Where useful, public indicators are defanged or described at a higher level. Extended supporting material is retained privately and can be shared selectively in a professional recruitment context.

## Research ethics

My underground monitoring is passive and defensive in purpose. The research does not imply affiliation, endorsement or operational participation in the activities of any group described in this portfolio.
